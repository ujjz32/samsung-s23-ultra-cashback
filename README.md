Three years after launch, the Galaxy S23 Ultra has turned into a strange kind of bargain. Samsung's newest Ultra costs four figures. ## What you're actually deciding

If you searched for this phone, you're probably in one of three situations:

- You want a flagship-grade camera and S Pen without paying S26 Ultra money
- You already own one and want to know how much life is left in it
- You're upgrading and trying to figure out whether to trade it in or sell it

Those three questions have different answers, and the software timeline is what ties them together.

## The part Samsung confirmed that changes the math

The Galaxy S23 line launched in February 2023 with Android 13 and a promise of four major OS upgrades. Samsung has now confirmed that **One UI 9, based on Android 17, will be the final major update** for the S23, S23+, S23 Ultra and S23 FE. The phones currently run One UI 8.5 on Android 16 QPR2, so that's three of four delivered, with one left.

After that, security patches continue for five years from launch — roughly through 2028 — but no new One UI versions. Samsung is also expected to skip the S23 series for One UI 9.5, the point release arriving with the Galaxy S27, which is exactly what happened to the S22 series when it got One UI 8 but never 8.5.

There's a second, less discussed cut. A batch of Galaxy AI tools — Photo Assist, Audio Eraser, Creative Studio, Call Screening — shipped with newer hardware and requires a Galaxy S24 or later. The S23 Ultra got substantial Galaxy AI features earlier (Generative Edit, Note Assist, Browsing Assist, Transcript Assist), but it won't get this newer set.

None of that makes the phone bad. It does mean that if you're buying today and plan to keep it four years, you're buying a device whose feature development is essentially finished.

> The S23 Ultra is still a capable phone in 2026. It's just a phone whose software story has an end date, and that date is close enough to matter when you're comparing prices.

## Should you buy the S23 Ultra or spend more?

Put it next to the phones it gets compared against.

Against the **S24 Ultra**, the S23 Ultra loses the newer AI toolkit, gets one fewer major OS update, and has a slightly curved screen. It keeps the 10x optical zoom, which the S24 Ultra replaced with a 5x lens. If zoom range is your thing, that one spec flips the comparison.

Against the **S26 Ultra**, you're looking at a real generational gap, not a marketing one. The newer phone gets features that will never reach the S23 Ultra, ships with One UI 8.5 and a seven-upgrade commitment, and will be supported years longer. It also costs considerably more, and Samsung's own trade-in math currently values an S23 Ultra at around $400 — down from $650 during a promotional window. Trade-in values for out-of-support Samsung phones have historically collapsed; the S22 Ultra's value illustrates the pattern.

So:

- **Buy the S23 Ultra** if you want flagship build, S Pen, 200MP main camera and 10x optical zoom, and you're comfortable with the software being effectively feature-frozen.
- **Buy the S24 Ultra or newer** if you keep phones beyond four years, or you want the newer AI features.
- **Don't buy at $800+.** At that price the case for a newer device gets hard to argue against.

## Which storage tier is worth the money

The gaps are smaller than you'd expect, which makes this an easy call.

The jump from 256GB to 512GB in the secondhand market runs about $75–$100. The jump from 512GB to 1TB runs another $80–$100, putting a 1TB unit at roughly 35% above a 256GB one.

The S23 Ultra has no microSD slot, so whatever you buy is what you live with. That argues for not scraping the bottom of the capacity ladder. But the 1TB premium buys a lot of cashback on a device whose resale value is already dropping, and most buyers never fill 512GB.

If you shoot a lot of 8K video or keep an offline library, take the 1TB. Otherwise 256GB is fine for a phone you'll realistically replace before it fills up, and 512GB is where most people land comfortably.

Cashback doesn't appear as spendable money immediately. It moves through four states:

1. **Tracked** — the click and order are logged, usually within hours.

3. **Confirmed** — the retailer's return window closed and the sale is verified.
4. **Withdrawable** — you can move it out.

The pending window is set by the retailer, not by ShopBack, because it mirrors that merchant's return and cancellation period. Retail orders generally clear in a few weeks.
